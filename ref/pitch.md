I have around nine years of experience in computer vision and perception systems. Although my applications have evolved—from satellite imagery and edge AI to industrial robotics—the common thread throughout my career has been solving perception problems that require reliable pose estimation, sensor fusion, and real-time decision making.

Over the last four years at Airbus, my work has shifted heavily toward 3D perception and robotics. I've been leading perception components involving RGB-D sensing, visual servoing, multi-sensor fusion, camera calibration, and real-time pose estimation for robotic aircraft assembly. One of the systems I built fuses high-frequency laser measurements with vision to control industrial robots at high precision for dexterous aicraft assembly tasks, and I am also leading the techno-brick of real-time 6D object pose estimation and tracking of novel objects.

While these are robotics applications, I realized that many of the underlying challenges are fundamentally the same as those in XR pose prediction, head tracking etc. Whether you're tracking a robot, an object, or a user's head, you're still solving problems around state estimation, SLAM, calibration, drift, jitter, sensor synchronization, and robustness under imperfect sensing conditions. The mathematics and the engineering principles are remarkably similar; the primary difference is that with robots its just a smooth and temporary trajectory divergence, but XR has much tighter user experience constraints, where even small perception errors become immediately noticeable to the user, thanks to our highly advanced vestibular feedback. 

What particularly excites me about this role is that it isn't purely algorithm development. It focuses on validating perception systems at scale—defining meaningful KPIs, designing automated validation pipelines, performing failure analysis, and helping algorithm teams understand why regressions occur. That's actually the part of perception engineering I enjoy the most. I like taking complex systems, making them observable, building rigorous validation frameworks, and using data to drive engineering decisions instead of intuition.

Over the past few years I've naturally gravitated toward that systems-level perspective, and I believe that's why this Staff Validation role is a strong fit for both my experience and the kind of engineer I've become."
